get started

what you share with the solstone app goes into your journal, on a computer you own. set up the journal first, then add the solstone app on each device you want to share from.

on your mac

an Apple silicon mac (M1 or newer) with macos 15 or later. Intel macs aren't supported.

  1. get both mac apps

    one download has the journal app and the solstone app. drag both to Applications.

    get both mac apps

  2. create your journal first

    open the journal app. choose where your journal lives, and lock in its mark: two shapes and two words that are your journal's own, so you can always tell which journal is yours. then choose open your journal to read it in your browser.

  3. connect the solstone app

    install and open the solstone app.open the solstone app. in settings, choose journal to connect. for a journal on your mac, confirm the one it finds. follow the prompts to choose what you share.

    get the solstone app

check it's ours. both mac apps are signed by sol pbc and notarized by Apple. the journal's signed release records are on trust.solstone.app.

prefer the terminal?

install both mac apps with one command:

curl -fsSL https://solstone.app/install.sh | sh -s -- --components all

for just one, replace all with journal or app. the installer verifies the download and puts the app in /Applications. updates stay inside each app.

installed the journal from the terminal before?

install the journal app, then open it. the journal app adopts an older journal only when it can verify the installation it is taking over. if anything is unclear, it stops and tells you what needs attention. your journal stays where it is. contact support if you need a hand.

journal on another computer?

in your journal, open network and choose pair a device. copy the pairing link. in the solstone app's journal settings, choose pair to a journal on another device, paste the link, and confirm it's your journal.

    run this command in your terminal. it installs your journal and starts it.

  2. add the desktop app

    the solstone app takes in what you share from your desktop, and all of it goes into your journal. its setup guide includes the packages your system needs.

2

what your device will ask

the solstone app only takes in the sources you turn on, and all of it goes into your journal. your device asks your permission the first time.

mac
the microphone for audio, and screen & system audio recording for your screen and the sound your mac plays. it also asks to find devices on your local network, so the solstone app can find your journal.
linux
your desktop asks which screen to share when you turn on your screen as a source.
windows
while our signing reputation builds, windows may warn that it couldn't verify the solstone app because it isn't commonly downloaded. the publisher it shows is sol pbc. after that, you choose what you share inside the solstone app. when you first open network in your journal to pair a device, windows asks whether to let it on your network; choose allow, so your devices can reach it.
iphone and ipad
the microphone and your location, each when you turn on the source that needs it; the camera, to scan your journal's pairing code; your local network, to reach your journal; and notifications, if you turn them on. the app asks for your precise location; you can choose approximate when it asks, or later in settings. your screen goes into your journal only while you broadcast it: you start it from the app, iphone shows its own screen-broadcast sheet, and you stop it there or from the red bar at the top of your screen.
android
the microphone and your location, each when you turn on the source that needs it; the camera, to scan your journal's pairing code or when you turn it on as a source; and notifications the first time you start sharing.

with audio on, the voices of people around you can go into your journal too, so let them know first. solstone and recording laws

you can pause any source, or turn it off, from the solstone app.

3

where does the thinking happen?

you choose during setup. your journal stays on your computer whichever you choose; what each choice sends, and to whom, is below.

local, the default

a model downloads and runs on your computer, which needs enough graphics memory for it.

your own model

bring your own provider key (Claude, Gemini, or GPT), or use an OpenAI-compatible endpoint you run. your provider sees what your journal sends it for thinking, under its own terms, and you pay it directly. sol pbc is never in the path.

confidential processing

think off your device on confidential hardware sol pbc runs, for $96 a year or $9.99 a month. no content is retained · no human reviews it · nothing is used to train. how it works

nothing is chosen for you silently. if no model is set, your journal tells you and waits.

4

connect your agent

the agents you already use can work from your journal. you choose which ones.

on the same computer

any agent that speaks MCP can connect to your journal from the same computer. through this connection, your agent can search and read your journal and keep notes of its own there. it can't add, change, or delete anything else.

  1. in your journal, open agents. on a mac or on windows it's also in the journal app, and on the journal's computer it's at localhost:5015/app/agents.
  2. choose connect an agent, then on this computer, then make a pairing code. the code works once and expires after 10 minutes.
  3. add your journal to your agent with the line the agents app shows for it. on every computer, that line points your agent at http://127.0.0.1:7659/mcp.
  4. your browser opens your journal's page. choose what the agent may see, enter the code, and connect.

on a mac or on windows, with Claude Code, the line is:

claude mcp add --transport http journal "http://127.0.0.1:7659/mcp"

then type /mcp in Claude Code, choose journal, and authenticate.

on linux, setting up your journal also adds a solstone skill for Claude Code, Codex and Gemini CLI, whether or not they're installed yet, so an agent you install later already has it. solstone skills list shows it. with the skill, an agent in any project can search your journal, look up a person, or read a transcript.

on your wifi or VPN

turn it on in your journal's agents app. an agent pairs with a code from your journal, and it only works on the route it paired through.

from anywhere

turn on solstone.me, an address for your journal that sol pbc runs, for $5 a year. or use a tunnel of your own: you never have to pay us.

your journal's agents app works with your own route or with solstone.me; solstone.me is the address, and the agents app is where you manage what connects.

6

day 1, and tomorrow morning

day 1

what you share goes into your journal as you go. open it in your browser to read your day. as your journal works through what came in, transcripts fill in, and with a model set in step 3, so do the people in them and your meetings.

tomorrow morning

a briefing for the day ahead is waiting in your journal, written from your own material by the model you chose. your agents can read it too.

updating your journal

update the journal on the computer that runs it. the solstone app and your journal update separately, so their version numbers can differ.

on mac: open the journal app and choose updates in its sidebar. the solstone app has its own updates pane in settings. manual journal update instructions.

on windows: open journal from the Start menu and manage updates there.

on linux: follow the journal upgrade guide.

after updating, reopen your journal and check the version in its status details.

early days. on a mac, on windows and on your phone, solstone installs like any app. on linux, setup takes a step in the terminal. expect a few rough edges, and tell us when you find one.
